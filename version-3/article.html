A day at the first Dublin GovJam

Mapping, talking to parents and a rough prototype, all in one day.

Leah Downing · November 2026 · 2 minute read

Event
Dublin GovJam, 3 November 2026
Hosted by
The design team at the Department of Justice, Home Affairs and Migration
Impact area
Public services
Written by
Leah Downing

On 3 November I took part in the first Dublin GovJam, hosted by the design team at the Department of Justice, Home Affairs and Migration. A GovJam brings people from the public and private sector together in teams to work on a public service problem, going from research to a rough prototype in a day.

My team worked on Fáilte Families (or Baby Meitheal, we haven’t picked a name yet), a service that matches new parents with other parents and trained parent ‘champions’ in their area.

We spoke to parents at playgrounds and at a maternity hospital. The first months with a new baby can be really isolating, especially if you live far from family or are still learning the language. It’s also when the mother’s post-natal check-ups end, at around six weeks, so a lot of the formal support stops while parents are still finding their feet. Often the most useful advice comes from other parents who have been through it, on the small questions you mightn’t feel comfortable asking a doctor.

Parents sign up with a few details, like language, area, cultural background and where they’d like to meet, and get matched with people nearby. The aim is that they build their own network of support that lasts well beyond the service.

I found it really interesting to see how the day was run, moving from mapping to talking to people on the street to a prototype. Thanks to the design team at the Department for hosting, and to my team for a great day.
