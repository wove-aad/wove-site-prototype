    What Irish design students told us about the future of education

    EducationFinding

    Karl Toomey, Labs Director

    3 Sep 2026 · 3 min read

    ShareEmail
    ThemeEducation
    WithIADT
    Written byKarl Toomey
    Date3 Sep 2026 · 3 min read

    Education is an area we care deeply about at Wove. Over the years we’ve worked with universities and educational bodies on large strategic projects, and many of us also lecture at colleges across Ireland and beyond.

    So earlier this year we spent 5 weeks with 3rd year Graphic Design students at IADT, giving them the tools to map their own educational journey, surface where it was breaking down, and prototype bold ideas in response.

    What they found

    The clearest finding was that the transitions between stages of education hurt the most. The CAO process came up again and again as chaotic and disorienting, and graduation brought its own whiplash: a sudden loss of structure, community and workspace.

    Within college itself, the dominant theme was confidence, or the lack of it: imposter syndrome, difficulty explaining the value of a design education to family, and constant self-doubt about “doing enough”.

    What they built

    The 14 provotypes clustered around a few clear responses: tools to make the CAO and Leaving Cert transition less opaque, peer support that reframes self-promotion and feedback as something done with classmates, and small interventions, a plant, a leaderboard, a booklet, to make campus life feel worth showing up for.

    These observations come from one cohort of design students in Dublin, so we wouldn’t claim they represent the national student experience. But the signal is strong enough that we think it’s worth sharing, and worth exploring with other courses and institutions.
