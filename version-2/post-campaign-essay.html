    Launching Circular.ie: the long version

    Project · Circular.ie · Brand

    How a national campaign for something people had never heard of came together, in full.

    Johnny Kelly, Managing Director

    29 Sep 2026 · 9 min read

    ShareEmail
    ClientCircular.ie
    Impact areaCircular economy
    TeamBrand
    PartnerTinyArk
    Part ofCircular.ie, a ten-year programme
    Talk toJohnny Kelly

    After months of co-creation workshops across Ireland, we had a clear brand strategy (“Uncomplicated Progress”) and a platform ready to go. But we needed a campaign that would cut through, reach diverse audiences, and establish foundational data for a 10-year learning project. Because at its heart, Circular.ie is a research initiative: everything is designed for testing, learning, and iterating what works.

    The campaign needed to speak to two distinct groups: citizens, people living circular lives, often without realising it, and communities, the groups and organisations already working on circular initiatives. We tailored radio ads, social content and digital advertising to each, showing individuals how circular actions connect to everyday life, while showing communities how their work fits within a national movement.

    We built the entire campaign around a simple idea.

    Show people they’re already doing it.

    The TV ads followed a deliberate progression. We started with single, highly relatable circular actions: filling a reusable water bottle, reheating last night’s dinner. Then we moved toward group actions that might be less familiar but still accessible: clothes swaps, tool libraries, community repair events. This structure validated what people already knew while expanding their understanding of what’s possible.

    Each scene was shot with dynamic camera movement and distinctive top-down perspectives that made everyday actions feel fresh and interesting. And in every single scene, the circular brand device appeared as a functional object: a bottle cap, a yellow wool rug, a bike tire, a lunchbox lid. Our circle became the driving part of the story.

    A major objective was generating foundational data on the website. The team had no baseline metrics, so the campaign needed to drive traffic and engagement while testing what resonated. On certain assets we ran different versions with male and female voiceovers to see if it impacted engagement rates. For social and digital content, we led with photography we’d shot of real people doing real things: deliberately less polished, more relatable, more grounded.

    Working with our production partners TinyArk, we delivered the campaign across national TV and on-demand, radio tailored to citizens and communities, digital advertising with A/B testing built in, and social content using real photography.

    Testing is still underway, but early indicators show the campaign is succeeding in making circular economy feel accessible and relevant to a mass audience, while establishing the data foundation needed for long-term learning.

    Behaviour change campaigns don’t need to rely on fear, guilt, or environmental messaging to be effective. They need to meet people where they are.
